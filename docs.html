401K

$401K is a coin with a plan administrator. Every thirty minutes a bot takes the coin's creator fees, buys the stocks that are already in your 401k, and sends them to every holder. That's the whole thing. The rest of this page is how each part of that sentence is enforced.

The stocks

Your 401k is, for most people, an S&P 500 fund. Open it and the top of the list is always the same: NVIDIA (8.08% of the index), Apple (7.03%), Microsoft (5.69%), Amazon (3.84%), Alphabet (3.01% and 2.40% across its two share classes), Broadcom (2.65%), Meta (1.90%), Micron (1.63%), Tesla (1.56%). Ten names, 37.79% of the whole index between them. That list is the plan. Not a basket somebody picked, not a stock somebody likes this week. The weights are the index's weights on the day, read from the index, and printed on the site with the date they were read.

The bot buys those ten as tokenized stocks, in those proportions. A name that doesn't exist as a tokenized stock yet is folded into $SPY itself, so the plan is never short a slice. The list and the weights are published before the first buy and every change to them is a dated line in the log.

The bot

The bot is one key with four rules it cannot break, printed here and in the code you can read.

Every thirty minutes, on the hour and the half, it claims the creator fees $401K has earned since the last cycle. If the total is below the cost of the swaps plus gas, it does nothing, writes "carried" in the log, and waits thirty minutes. If it clears the bar, it quotes each stock first, refuses any route that delivers less than the quote minus a published guard, dry-runs the swap, and only then buys. Before each cycle it posts what it is about to buy and why, so the reasoning is on record before the money moves, not after.

It never sells. It cannot sell. It has no function for it.

Payday, every thirty minutes

The moment the buys land, the bot sends the stocks out to holders pro rata by balance at that block. Not a claim, not a form, not a dashboard balance that says you own something. The tokenized shares, in your wallet, with the transaction hash beside your address in the cycle's ledger. Hold $401K at the block and NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft and the rest arrive in your wallet forty-eight times a day, in whatever size the last thirty minutes of trading paid for.

A 401k at work does this every second Friday with your salary. This one does it every thirty minutes with the trade tape, and it doesn't wait for you to retire.

What a cycle looks like

Fees claimed. Ten quotes. Ten buys, or one buy of $SPY if the amount is too small to split ten ways without losing it to gas. One distribution per stock to every holder above the dust line. One log entry, signed by the bot's key, that lists the amount in, the amount of each stock out, every transaction hash, and the gas it cost. The site shows the last cycle, a countdown to the next, and a ledger of every cycle since the first. You can run one yourself on the home page with rehearsal fees; it does every step above except the two that move money, and it says so.

What the bot keeps

Nothing. Gas comes out of the cycle and is printed on the cycle. There is no treasury, no reserve, no "compounding" line that quietly keeps a slice. The bot's wallet is printed on the site so anyone can watch it, and its balance between cycles should be close to zero. If it isn't, the log says why, or the plan is over.

What the dev keeps

The dev never had the fees. They are wired to the bot's wallet at launch, and the wiring can't be moved afterwards, not by the dev and not by us. The dev holds $401K like anyone else and gets stocks like anyone else. Dumping shrinks the next cycle for everyone, including the dev.

The four numbers

The home page shows four numbers and refuses to show any others: cycles, fees in, stocks bought, holders paid. Cycles is how many times the bot has run. Fees in is the total it has ever claimed. Stocks bought is the total it has spent on the ten. Holders paid is how many wallets have received stock at least once. Every number is read from the chain. If it can't be, it isn't on the page.

What is not built yet

No cycles yet. The first runs thirty minutes after the coin is live and the first fee has landed. The site today shows the plan with the real index data of the day, the ten names, their weights and prices, and a rehearsal of a cycle that is tagged as a rehearsal. The buys and the payouts are the two things you will never have to take our word for, because both are transactions with hashes. The bot's schedule is the one thing you trust us on until the cron is on-chain, and the log is how you check.

The hard questions

Why would I hold?

Because every thirty minutes you are holding, real stocks land in your wallet, and they are yours whether $401K goes up, down or sideways.

Why these stocks?

Because they are the ones already in your 401k. The boring part of investing is the product. That is also the joke.

What if the dev dumps?

The dev never had the fees. Selling just makes his own next cycle smaller.

Can the bot rug?

It has no sell function, no withdrawal function, and one key boxed by four published rules and a public log. The worst it can do is stop, and stopping shows in the countdown.

What if a cycle is tiny?

It carries. The log says "carried" and the next cycle is bigger. Nothing is lost between cycles.
